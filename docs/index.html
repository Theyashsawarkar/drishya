Dṛśya (दृश्य) is Sanskrit for "sight, what is seen". It's the sibling of nāda ("sound"), the music player: same keys, same look.

#Features

  • Video first: mpv draws into the window (libmpv, hardware decoding). The controls float over the video and fade after a moment; the timeline shows the chapters, what's buffered, and a preview frame where you hover. f is fullscreen as zen: just the video, and a small readout in the corner when you seek or press i. t theater, z a mini player.
  • Search from anywhere (/): one floating panel for YouTube, your videos, your history, a link you paste and commands. Your own videos answer as you type, YouTube a moment later, the best match first; Tab picks where to look. Play, play next, add or download without leaving it.
  • YouTube without an account: every result with its thumbnail, channel, views and length; related videos play after yours (autoplay, M).
  • Any link: o and paste. HLS (.m3u8) and DASH streams from any server, a video file over HTTP, live streams, and the sites yt-dlp plays. A stream's qualities are capped like YouTube's.
  • Signs in when it has to: a server that asks for a username and password gets a sign-in panel; sites that need an account can borrow your browser's login. Logins you choose to remember go to your keyring.
  • Your library as a grid: Continue watching first, then your folders; inside, the episodes in order (S01E02, "Episode 10" after "Episode 9"), thumbnails, and the next episode lined up after the one you play.
  • Resume and history: every video starts where you stopped. History (4) has a Continue watching list, with a red bar for how much you've seen.
  • Up next, edited like text: dd removes, J/K move, yy/p copy and paste, V selects several, u undoes, Ctrl+r redoes.
  • One settings menu (Q): quality, speed, subtitles, audio track, download. Quality lists what YouTube offers for this video (4K down to 144p) or a stream's own qualities. Your pick stays for the next videos and for downloads.
  • Downloads with subtitles: D (or the download button) saves a video (YouTube or any link) at your quality, with its subtitles, chapters and thumbnail; it then plays from your disk.
  • Subtitles, audio tracks, speed, chapters: c, #, [ ], { }. Subtitles are real captions in your languages, never YouTube's machine translations.
  • Everywhere on the desktop: MPRIS for media keys, playerctl and your phone (KDE Connect); drishya ctl and drishya play for scripts.
  • In your theme: varna's colours when you have it, the glass of Vyom's panels; Catppuccin Mocha without.

#Install

#Requirements

  • Python 3.11+, PyGObject, GTK 4
  • mpv (drishya uses its library, libmpv, which comes with it)
  • yt-dlp for YouTube and other sites, ffmpeg for thumbnails and downloads
  • optional: secret-tool (libsecret) to remember logins in your keyring, brightnessctl for the brightness keys, a Nerd Font for the icons
# Arch
sudo pacman -S --needed python-gobject gtk4 mpv yt-dlp ffmpeg libsecret ttf-jetbrains-mono-nerd
# Debian / Ubuntu
sudo apt install python3-gi gir1.2-gtk-4.0 libmpv2 yt-dlp ffmpeg libsecret-tools

#drishya

git clone https://github.com/Theyashsawarkar/drishya ~/.local/share/drishya
ln -s ~/.local/share/drishya/bin/drishya ~/.local/bin/drishya
drishya            # opens the player (it starts itself the first time)

For app launchers and to open video files with it:

ln -s ~/.local/share/drishya/data/dev.vayu.Drishya.desktop ~/.local/share/applications/
xdg-mime default dev.vayu.Drishya.desktop video/mp4 video/x-matroska video/webm

Update with git -C ~/.local/share/drishya pull && drishya quit.

To start it with your session (so it opens at once), a user service:

# ~/.config/systemd/user/drishya.service
[Unit]
PartOf=graphical-session.target
After=graphical-session.target
[Service]
ExecStart=%h/.local/bin/drishya daemon
Restart=on-failure
[Install]
WantedBy=graphical-session.target

On a tiling compositor, float the mini player in a corner. sway:

for_window [app_id="^dev\.vayu\.Drishya$" title="^drishya mini$"] floating enable, sticky enable, resize set 480 270, move position 1420 790

#Usage

drishya                     open the player, or close it if it's open
drishya play <what>         play now: a YouTube link, any stream or video link, a file or folder,
                            or search words (plays the top YouTube result)
drishya add [--next] <what> add to Up next instead
drishya show | hide | mini  the window, hidden, the mini player
drishya tab <name>          watch queue library youtube history downloads
drishya ctl <command>       toggle play pause next prev | seek +10 | seek 1:30 | vol 80 | speed 1.5
drishya status [--json]     what's playing
drishya quit                stop it

Tabs: 0 Watch, 1 Up next, 2 Library, 3 History, 4 Downloads; / searches YouTube, your videos and history from any of them. Lists move with j/k; Enter plays. ? shows every key.

#Keys

The footer always shows the keys for where you are; ? shows them all. The same rules everywhere: / searches, Esc goes back or closes, j/k move, h/l go back and in (menus, the grid), Enter does the main thing, Tab switches section; while typing, Ctrl+j/Ctrl+k move. Counts work like vim: 5j, 3.. A tab number followed by a key that takes no count (or a lone 1) goes to that tab first.

Volume and brightness: =/- change the system volume and Ctrl+=/Ctrl+- the screen's brightness (pactl or wpctl, and brightnessctl); when the window closes, both go back to how they were.

#Links, streams and signing in

Press o, paste a link and Enter (or paste it into the YouTube search box, or run drishya play <link>). drishya plays:

  • HLS and DASH (.m3u8, .mpd) from any server, live or not. A stream that offers several qualities plays the best one up to your quality.
  • A video file on a server (https://…/film.mkv), streamed as it plays.
  • Any site yt-dlp supports (Vimeo, Twitch, Dailymotion, news sites…).
  • rtmp://, rtsp://, srt:// streams.

When the server answers that it needs a login (HTTP 401 or 403), a sign-in panel opens: your username and password, sent as HTTP authentication with every request, including each segment of a stream. Tick Remember in my keyring (Ctrl+s) to keep it in your keyring (through libsecret), else it's kept only until drishya quits. A site that needs an account offers your browser's login instead (Ctrl+b: yt-dlp reads its cookies). A link with the login in it (https://user:password@host/…) works too: the login is taken out of the link, so it never reaches the history or the queue file.

#Configuration

~/.config/drishya/config.toml, everything optional:

[drishya]
quality = 1080           # highest resolution to stream or download
autoplay = true          # more after your videos: related ones, or the next episode
resume = true            # start where you stopped
seek_step = 10           # seconds for , and .
fine_step = 5            # seconds for h and l on the Watch tab
volume_step = 5
subtitles = "en"         # your subtitle languages, e.g. "en,hi"
video_dirs = ["~/Videos"]
download_dir = "~/Videos/YouTube"
hwdec = "auto-copy-safe" # mpv's hardware decoding; "no" to turn it off
pause_on_hide = true     # q pauses (z keeps a mini player going)
# opacity = 0.62         # the window's glass (unset: your theme's)

Styles: ~/.config/drishya/style.css is loaded after drishya's own. State: ~/.local/state/drishya (history, queue, settings, downloads); thumbnails: ~/.cache/drishya.

#How it works

  • One resident process (drishya daemon, the app id dev.vayu.Drishya) holds the player, MPRIS and downloads; drishya only sends it a D-Bus message, so the window opens at once.
  • The player is libmpv, bound with ctypes (drishya/mpv.py): no extra Python package. Its OpenGL renderer draws into a GTK GLArea; the mini player takes the renderer over and gives it back.
  • YouTube plays through mpv's own yt-dlp support, which picks the streams (no AV1, which few laptops decode in hardware) and renews them. Search, related videos and downloads call yt-dlp directly, never on the GTK main loop.
  • Logs go to stderr: journalctl --user -u drishya with the service. DRISHYA_DEBUG=1 shows debug messages.

#Troubleshooting

  • Black video, sound plays. Look at journalctl --user -u drishya for "OpenGL" or "video output". hwdec = "no" rules out hardware decoding.
  • YouTube says "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot". The sign-in panel offers your browser's login (Ctrl+b).
  • A stream won't play. Try it in mpv (mpv '<link>'); if mpv can't, drishya can't either. A 403 often means the link expired: get a fresh one.
  • No thumbnails for your files. Install ffmpeg.
